The textbook, minus the textbook

Most economics courses start with graphs and end with confusion. Taconomics starts at a taco stand instead, and never really leaves it. Supply and demand, market equilibrium, production costs, pricing strategy, competition — every chapter is the same stand, one variable changed at a time.

It's built for students stuck on the theory, small business owners pricing their own product, and anyone who's wondered why the taco truck raises its prices on game day. Author Sergio Nieto strips the jargon out and leaves the reasoning in, so the ideas stick because you can picture exactly where they came from.

By the last page, you're not just able to define market equilibrium — you can watch it happen, one taco at a time.

About the author

Frustrated with how economics gets taught, Sergio Nieto built a different approach: explain the theory through one taco stand instead of a stack of unrelated case studies. He calls it the Taco Stand Economics method, and it's been the backbone of his classroom for over a decade.

The method has since made its way into conferences and workshops, and Sergio is available to speak on economics and economic history for schools, businesses, and events.
